How do you play?

To play, you'll need to go to the 'Play' page of the website. There you'll see three options, which set the difficulty: Casual (the song name is mentioned in the clip), Familiar (the song clip will have lyrics), and Worshiper (the song clip won't have lyrics, or will only contain a few).

Next, you'll need to pick how many songs you want to guess in the game. The options are Singularity (1), Trinity (3), and Quintessence (5).

After that, you'll be in the game, guessing the song. At first, what you see can be confusing, but it's really simple once you know what everything does. The text at the top is just the difficulty level; the three 'X's are your strikes (how many wrong guesses you can make); and below the strikes are the song indicators. These tell you not only how many songs you have to guess but also whether you correctly guessed a song, skipped it, got it wrong, or whether it's the song you're currently guessing. The sound controls are below the song indicators and have four actions: Play/Pause, Replay/Repeat Song, Skip Song, and Submit Guess. Below that is the song progress bar, which shows how much time is left on the song. Lastly is the search bar/drop-down menu: if you click the search bar, a drop-down menu will appear with all the songs.

Now that you understand what you see, you'll need to actually guess a song. There are around 113 songs from 47 artists in the rotation (there is a chance I'll add more). The entire game consists of listening to the audio clip and trying to guess the song, with the difficulty increasing from Casual to Worshiper.

What are the 'Song Indicators'?

The song indicators tell you the state of each song. They also tell you how many songs you need to guess. There are five indicators: blue - song selected/you're on, green - guessed correctly, orange - skipped, grey/very dark blue - song unseen, and a hidden red colour which you can't see because it brings up the end-game screen.

What are Strikes?

Strikes are the number of mistakes or wrong guesses you can make before you lose. Once all three strikes turn red, the game ends and you'll go to the end-game screen to see how well you did. Strikes stay consistent at three, so even if you play a game with five songs, you will always have three strikes.

What songs are there?

All the songs in rotation can be seen with the links on the 'Song List' page. The songs are based on the songs in my Spotify playlist, which I have gathered over the years. Some "wild cards" out there are songs from "New Zealand Bands", like Alabaster Worship, Eastgate Worship (my church), Te Rautini, and Souvenirs Worship. That's because I'm from New Zealand and they're great songs from where I'm from.

Why did I make this?

After making a Stratagem Hero clone (the Helldivers 2 arcade game), Blackjack, and Ride the Bus with a physical chip system in Python using Pygame, I wanted to move to a different language and try a different idea: a gambling game. I wanted to base this game on something to do with songs, specifically CCM (Contemporary Christian Music). I've played games like Songdle and I've found it very difficult because I had no clue what the majority of the songs on there were. So, I decided to make a game where you need to guess a CCM song based on limited information given. I also do something similar when listening to music on Spotify, where I try to guess what the song is based on the start.

Notes about the game

Hovering over the difficulty options will tell you how "hard" they are.

When guessing the song, it is a good idea to search for the song and select it in the drop-down menu. This will prevent problems with spelling mistakes and songs with the same name.

You can go through the drop-down menu with the arrow keys and Tab to go down.

Audio should play automatically when entering the game, so in case it doesn't, just press the play or repeat button.

You can pause the audio using the big pause/play button and replay (repeat) the song using the repeat button.

You can skip a song that you're unsure about by pressing the skip button. There are no repercussions for skipping songs, but you'll eventually need to guess them.
